Chapter one

About this guide

Why this guide was prepared, which products it covers and who is responsible for it.

1Introduction

This guide reflects the commitment of the Directorate General for Standards and Metrology to strengthening consumer protection, raising awareness of the safety and quality requirements for gas appliances and their accessories, and helping consumers make sound purchasing decisions based on clear and reliable information.

Appliances that run on liquefied petroleum gas (LPG), such as cookers, water heaters and space heaters, together with accessories such as gas cylinders, valves, regulators and hoses, need particular care when they are chosen, installed, used and maintained, because of the risks that can arise if they do not meet safety requirements or are used incorrectly.

This guide explains the key conformity requirements, product labels and certificates of conformity, and how to check that products are safe and conforming, with practical advice to help consumers recognise conforming products before and after buying them.

2Scope of the guide

This guide aims to inform consumers in the Sultanate of Oman about the essential requirements for choosing and buying appliances that run on liquefied petroleum gas (LPG) and their accessories, and for checking their conformity with the approved technical regulation. It covers the following products; tap any category for details:

Rigid gas cylinders and tanks Composite (fibreglass) cylinders Accessories for gas appliances Domestic cooking appliances Commercial cooking appliances Heating appliances Water heaters
This guide is an awareness document for consumers. It does not replace the technical regulation, the standards or the related national legislation.

General definitions

For the purposes of this guide, the following terms have the meanings given next to each. Search for any term:

Liquefied petroleum gas (LPG)
A mixture of petroleum gases, such as propane and butane, used as fuel for cooking, heating and water heating.
Appliance
Any appliance designed to operate on liquefied petroleum gas, such as cookers, heaters and water heaters.
Accessories
Equipment and devices used with gas appliances, such as valves, regulators, hoses and safety devices.
Gas cylinder
A container designed for filling and storing liquefied petroleum gas under pressure.
Cylinder valve
A device fitted to the cylinder to open and close the gas outlet.
Gas regulator
A device that controls the pressure and flow of gas from the cylinder to the appliance.
Certificate of conformity
A document issued by a notified conformity assessment body proving that the product meets the specified technical requirements.
Gulf Conformity Mark
A mark showing that the product meets the requirements of the relevant Gulf technical regulations.
Product label
The data and information shown on the product, its packaging or the accompanying documents to identify it and its use and safety requirements.
Quick Response code (QR Code)
A code that can be read with a smartphone to reach electronic information about the product or to trace its conformity.
Economic operator
The manufacturer, authorised representative, importer or distributor responsible for the product under the technical regulation.
Conformity assessment
The procedures used to verify that the product meets the specified technical requirements.
Flame supervision devices (FSD)
Safety devices that detect the flame and shut off the gas supply when it goes out, according to the appliance’s design and technical requirements.

Chapter two · Question 6

What should you consider before buying?

Choosing the right appliance is an important step for your safety and your family’s. Your decision should not rest on price or appearance alone, but on technical and practical factors.

First: study your needs and means carefully

Purpose of use

Decide what it is for: cooking, heating, water heating or something else.

Place of use

Indoors or outdoors, with adequate ventilation according to the manufacturer’s instructions.

Gas type

Check that the appliance is designed for the gas you have: propane, butane or a mixture.

Gas pressure

The appliance pressure must match your gas supply and the regulator used.

Capacity and output

Choose what meets your needs without oversizing.

Dimensions and space

Check that the appliance fits the space where it will be installed.

Running and maintenance

Gas consumption, maintenance costs and spare parts.

Safety requirements

Protection and safety features suited to the type of appliance.

Second: compare the appliances that suit your needs

Once you know your needs, compare the products on the market using these criteria:

Remember: The cheapest product is not necessarily the best choice; safety and technical conformity should be among your top criteria when buying.
  • A valid certificate of conformity for the product under the applicable technical requirements.
  • The Gulf Conformity Mark, where required.
  • A clear, legible product label.
  • Operating, installation and maintenance instructions in Arabic, or in Arabic and English.
  • Appropriate safety devices and equipment.
  • Clear details of the manufacturer and importer and how to contact them.
  • Warranty, after-sales service and spare parts.
  • A price that matches the product’s quality and technical specifications.

Certificate of conformity and product label

The two documents that tell you a lot about a product before you pay.

7What is a certificate of conformity?

An official document issued by a notified conformity assessment body after the necessary evaluation and testing against the relevant technical requirements and standards. It confirms that the product has gone through the specified conformity assessment procedures and meets the technical requirements that apply to it.

  • It provides documented proof that the product went through conformity assessment.
  • It helps confirm that the product meets safety requirements.
  • It builds consumer confidence in products on the market.
  • It helps market surveillance authorities verify product conformity.
  • It limits the circulation of non-conforming products that may endanger consumers, property or the environment.

9Can I see the certificate of conformity before I buy?

Yes

You can ask the seller or supplier to show you the certificate of conformity before buying, and you have the right to ask whether the appliance or accessory conforms to the technical regulation and the relevant standards.

Consumer tip: Do not rely on the words “approved” or “meets the standards” in an advertisement alone; ask for proof of conformity.

It is advisable to:

  1. Ask for a copy of the certificate of conformity or its key details.
  2. Check that the certificate covers the exact model you want to buy.
  3. Make sure it was issued by a notified conformity assessment body.
  4. Check the certificate’s status and validity through the official channels available.
  5. Make sure it covers the product and accessories concerned, not a different product.

10What is a product label?

A set of data and information the manufacturer must provide on the product, its packaging or the accompanying documents, so that consumers know the product, its characteristics, its origin and how to use it safely. It helps you identify the product and its model, the manufacturer and importer, the country of origin, the right gas type and operating pressure, the warnings and safety requirements, and trace the product when needed.

12Must every new product carry a label?

Yes

The required label data must be provided for new products covered by the technical regulation, according to the nature of the product and the annex on marking requirements. The information must be correct, clear and not misleading to consumers.

When the product is smallIf the size or nature of the product does not allow all the data to be shown on it, some may be placed on the packaging or in an accompanying leaflet, with a clear note that more information is in the accompanying documents.
Do not buy a new product that lacks essential data, or whose manufacturer, origin or safe-use requirements cannot be identified.

Chapter six · Questions 22 to 28

Safety and safe use

From the flame supervision device inside the appliance to the cylinder, regulator and hose, installation, what to do in a leak, maintenance and your rights.

Important safety advice · Question 26

What should I do if I suspect a gas leak?

Treat a gas leak seriously: gas building up in enclosed spaces can cause a fire or an explosion.

  1. 1Do not light matches, lighters or any other source of flame.
  2. 2Do not switch on or off electrical switches or electronic devices inside the area.
  3. 3Shut off the gas supply if you can do so safely.
  4. 4Open doors and windows for ventilation if you can do so without putting yourself at risk.
  5. 5Leave the area and move away from the source of the leak.
  6. 6Call the competent authorities or emergency services from a safe place.
  7. 7Do not go back until the danger is over and the competent authorities allow it.

22Why do flame supervision devices (FSD) matter?

Flame on, gas flowing

Safety devices that check the flame stays lit in gas appliances and shut off the gas flow when it goes out, helping to prevent gas from leaking and building up.

They are a mandatory safety requirement for the domestic and commercial appliances covered, and must be tested to confirm they shut off the gas supply when the flame goes out.

  • Make sure the appliance has a flame supervision system as required.
  • The safety system must be part of the appliance design, not an unapproved add-on.
  • Never tamper with or disable safety devices.
  • Have maintenance and checks done by a qualified technician.

23Precautions when buying a gas cylinder
  • Buy from licensed suppliers and outlets.
  • Check the cylinder body for cracks, bulges or visible damage.
  • Check that the valve is sound, with no damage or corrosion at the connection.
  • Check that the required label data and markings are present.
  • Make sure the cylinder is intended for the gas type and use required.
  • Never use a cylinder of unknown origin or unknown condition.
  • Never try to repair the cylinder or its valve yourself.
  • Make sure the required periodic inspections have been carried out under the applicable rules and standards.
24Choosing the regulator and hose
  • Choose a regulator that matches the gas type and the operating pressure the appliance needs.
  • Use hoses intended for LPG.
  • Check the hose for cracks, damage or bulges.
  • Make sure the connections are suitable and tight.
  • Never use hoses or regulators of unknown origin.
  • Replace damaged parts according to the manufacturer’s instructions, by a qualified technician.
25Precautions when installing gas appliances

Installation must follow the manufacturer’s instructions and the approved technical requirements, taking the following into account:

  • Use a qualified installer.
  • Make sure the location suits the appliance.
  • Provide adequate ventilation as the appliance requires.
  • Make sure the gas type and supply pressure match the appliance.
  • Check that all connections and joints are sound.
  • Check for gas leaks after installation with a suitable test method.
  • Make sure the flue system for combustion products is sound, if the appliance needs one.
  • Never modify the appliance or its parts against the manufacturer’s instructions.
27Why regular maintenance matters

Regular maintenance keeps appliances safe and efficient and reduces the risk of faults and gas leaks. Consumers should:

  • Follow the maintenance schedule set by the manufacturer.
  • Have periodic checks done by a qualified technician.
  • Stop using appliances that show signs of damage or faults.
  • Check that burners are sound and the flame is stable.
  • Keep ventilation openings clear and never block them.
  • Never disable or bypass safety devices.
  • Keep the user manual, certificate of conformity, warranty and maintenance records.

28What are my rights if I find a non-conforming product?

  1. Stop using itif the product is dangerous.
  2. Keep the documentsThe purchase invoice, warranty and product documents.
  3. Record the product detailsModel, serial number and the manufacturer’s or importer’s name.
  4. Inform the seller or supplierof the problem.
  5. File a reportwith the consumer protection authorities or the relevant market surveillance bodies.
  6. Cooperate with the authoritiesand provide the information and documents that help verify the complaint.
Don’t hesitate to report products you suspect do not conform; reporting protects consumers and limits the circulation of unsafe products.
